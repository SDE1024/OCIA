# Organized Outline: “The Holy Sacrifice of the Mass”
**Speaker:** Chris Mars, Director of Faith Formation, Holy Family Catholic Church, Orange, CA (Diocese of Orange)  
**Topic:** Understanding, preparing for, and actively participating in the Mass  
**Format:** Talk with opening/closing prayer; live Q&A omitted from transcript

---

## 1. Opening and Purpose (0:00–1:23)
- Welcome and introduction.
- Goal: explain the importance of the Mass, the listener’s role in it, and key parts that are often misunderstood.
- Opening prayer: asks for open hearts and minds to understand what we do, why we do it, and how to “maximize” the Mass for God first and for us second.

## 2. Thesis: The Eucharist Is the Source and Summit (1:23–4:24)
- Phrase comes from the *Catechism* and Vatican II: the Eucharist is the source and summit of the Christian life.
- All sacraments and ecclesiastical ministries are bound up in and oriented toward the Eucharist.
- The Eucharist contains the whole spiritual good of the Church: Christ himself.
- Common objections:
  - Isn’t the Incarnation the summit?
  - Isn’t the Sermon on the Mount?
  - Isn’t Christ’s Passion?
  - Isn’t the Resurrection?
- Answer: The Eucharist is the summit because Jesus is truly present—body, blood, soul, and divinity—and gives himself substantially to us in intimate communion.
- Purpose of Eucharist: healing, strengthening, transformation, and unification with Christ and one another.

## 3. Biblical Preparation for the Eucharist (4:24–9:04)
God prepared humanity for the Eucharist through salvation history:
- **Melchizedek** offers bread and wine; Christ is priest in the order of Melchizedek.
- **Abraham and Isaac**: willing sacrifice; God says he will provide—foreshadowing the Son.
- **Passover**: deliverance from Egypt; connection to Last Supper and Mass.
- **Manna in the desert**: God provides bread from heaven daily.
- **Temple worship**: sacrifice for atonement.
- **Synagogue worship**: hearing and studying God’s word.
  - These correspond to the Liturgy of the Eucharist and Liturgy of the Word.
- **Malachi**: one pure sacrifice offered worldwide daily.
- **Bethlehem** means “House of Bread”; Jesus is laid in a manger—food for animals—symbolizing spiritual food.

## 4. Jesus’ Teaching: John 6 and the Institution (9:04–17:34)
### A. Feeding of the 5,000
- Shows Jesus can make the finite infinite; no worry about running out.
### B. Walking on Water
- Jesus changes the nature of water without changing its appearance—prefiguring transubstantiation.
### C. Bread of Life Discourse (John 6)
- Jesus says “unless you eat my flesh and drink my blood” seven times.
- Greek words become more graphic: eating/gnawing/chewing.
- Audience understood him literally and many disciples left.
- Jesus does not stop them or say “I’m only speaking symbolically.”
- Peter’s response: “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.”
  - Lesson: We do not need to understand scientifically; we trust that God will do it.
### D. Last Supper
- Jesus institutes the Eucharist: “This is my body… This is my blood… Do this in remembrance of me.”
- “Remembrance” means entering into the actual event, not merely recalling it.
- This is a command, not a suggestion.
- Great Commission: “I am with you always”—fulfilled in the Eucharist.
- Three great commands:
  1. Become masters of love.
  2. Go make disciples.
  3. Do this in memory of me.

## 5. Why Transubstantiation Is Reasonable (17:34–21:49)
- Jesus is not physically present on the altar; he remains in heaven.
- The change is **substantial**, not physical: the substance becomes Christ’s body, blood, soul, and divinity; appearances remain bread and wine.
- God created everything from nothing and sustains the universe; changing substance is easy for him.
- Incarnation is a greater mystery: Jesus is fully divine and fully human.
- Miracles—water into wine, healings, walking on water—show Jesus’ authority over nature.
- “Take Peter’s advice”: He is God; we are not. We trust his word.

## 6. Emmaus and the Shape of the Mass (21:49–26:10)
- After the Resurrection, Jesus’ first act is to celebrate a Mass-like encounter on the road to Emmaus.
- He begins with the Liturgy of the Word: Moses and the prophets, explanation/homily.
- Then at table: takes bread, blesses, breaks, gives—their eyes are opened in the Eucharist.
- They immediately go back to Jerusalem to share the good news—the model for the dismissal.

## 7. Why We Get Bored and What Mass Is (26:10–28:44)
- “Mass confusion”: many Catholics are expected to understand the Mass by osmosis.
- Mass is not entertainment, education, or a social event.
- We are not an audience; we are active participants and kingdom priests.
- Mass is primarily for God: worship, thanksgiving, adoration, atonement, petition.
- *Liturgy* means “work of the people”—we go to Mass to work.
- Four encounters with God:
  1. Through each other.
  2. Through the priest.
  3. Through the Word.
  4. Through Christ’s real presence.
- We offer worship, thanksgiving, adoration, atonement, and petition.

## 8. Preparation Before Mass (28:44–32:37)
- Be reconciled with others before offering your gift (Matt 23:24).
- Be in a worthy state: free from mortal sin; use confession.
- Believe what the Church teaches, especially the Real Presence.
- Read and meditate on the readings before Mass.
- Know your intentions: what are you offering, asking, and aligning with God’s will?
- Cultivate a prayerful state.
- Fast one hour before Communion.
- Dress reverently—offer your best to God.
- Arrive early to calm down and enter prayer.

## 9. Handouts Mentioned (32:37–34:51)
1. **Order of the Mass**—what happens, physical responses, spiritual responses.
2. **What’s in Church**—names and purposes of objects in the church and sacristy.
3. **Silent Prayers**—priest’s vesting prayers and 12 silent prayers during Mass.

## 10. Walkthrough of the Mass (34:51–1:02:35)

### A. Introductory Rites
- **Entrance procession**: reenacts Palm Sunday and Christ’s entrance into Jerusalem; begins Passion Week.
- **Sign of the Cross**: a pledge dedicating the next hour to God.
- **Greeting**: “The Lord be with you” / “And with your spirit.”
  - “With your spirit” asks God to empower the celebrant to act *in persona Christi*.
- **Penitential Act**: reflect on sin, repent, ask for strength.
- **Gloria**: praise God for forgiveness; words echo Scripture.
- **Collect**: first intentional delay.
  - Bring your petitions to mind while the Roman Missal is brought to the priest.
  - The priest “collects” all petitions and offers them to God.

### B. Liturgy of the Word
- Sit to focus.
- There are **five readings**, not three:
  1. First Reading (usually Old Testament).
  2. Responsorial Psalm.
  3. Second Reading (usually New Testament Epistle).
  4. Alleluia verse.
  5. Gospel.
- First Reading and Gospel are thematically linked.
- Stand for the Gospel.
- **Homily** = explanation; priest is required to comment on the readings, not arbitrary topics.
- **Creed**: ends with “Amen” = “I believe.” Do not say it if you do not believe.
- **Prayers of the Faithful**: communal petitions; completes Liturgy of the Word.

### C. Liturgy of the Eucharist
- **Collection and preparation of gifts**: second intentional delay.
  - Purpose: offer yourself completely with Jesus on the altar—good, bad, ugly.
- **Invitation to prayer**: priest prays that his sacrifice and ours may be acceptable.
- **Eucharistic Prayer**:
  - “The Lord be with you”; “Lift up your hearts”; “Let us give thanks.”
  - Preface.
  - Epiclesis and Institution Narrative.
  - **Transubstantiation** occurs: bread and wine become Christ’s substantial presence.
  - Memorial Acclamation: “The mystery of faith.”
  - Doxology and **Great Amen**.

### D. Communion Rite
- **Lord’s Prayer**: seven petitions—three for God, four for us.
  - Most important line: forgive us as we forgive others.
- **Sign of Peace**: symbolic forgiveness; reenacts Jesus’ Easter “Peace be with you.”
- **Fraction**: priest places a piece of the host into the chalice—symbol of Resurrection.
- **Agnus Dei**: “Lamb of God.”
- **“Lord, I am not worthy…”**: humility; worthiness comes from Christ.
- **Third intentional delay**: prepare for Communion; reflect on the gift.
- **Communion**: unlike food, we do not change the Eucharist into us—we are changed to be more like Christ.
  - Effects: healing, strength, transformation, unification.
- **Fourth intentional delay**: silence after Communion; God is intimately present.

### E. Concluding Rites
- Prayer after Communion.
- Final blessing—do not leave early and miss it.
- Dismissal: *Ite, missa est*; “Mass” comes from *missa*, “go forth.”
- We are sent to fulfill the three commands: love, make disciples, do this in memory of me.

## 11. Liturgy Wars, Intercommunion, and Continuity (1:02:35–1:07:06)
- Debates about extraordinary vs. ordinary form, Latin vs. Novus Ordo, miss the point.
- Core elements are the same in every rite.
- Justin Martyr’s mid-2nd-century *First Apology* describes:
  - Sunday gathering.
  - Readings from apostles and prophets.
  - Homily/exhortation.
  - Prayers.
  - Offering of bread, wine, and water.
  - Eucharistic prayer and Great Amen.
  - Distribution/Communion.
- The Mass’s core structure has not changed in 2,000 years.
- Communion is restricted to Catholics in full communion as an act of love:
  - Because the Church believes it is truly Christ’s body, blood, soul, and divinity.
  - Receiving without belief/full communion would be harmful.

## 12. Wrap-Up and Closing Prayer (1:07:06–1:10:23)
- The Eucharist is the source and summit because it is Jesus himself.
- We were prepared for it through salvation history, taught by Jesus, and commanded to do it.
- The command is for our good: it empowers us to love and make disciples.
- We participate as kingdom priests, offering ourselves with Christ.
- We encounter God through each other, the priest, the Word, and the Real Presence.
- Communion transforms us to become more like Christ.
- Closing prayer and blessing.

---

# Key Takeaways
1. **The Mass is not for entertainment; it is worship offered to God.**
2. **The Eucharist is the Real Presence—body, blood, soul, and divinity—under the appearances of bread and wine.**
3. **We are active participants and kingdom priests, not an audience.**
4. **Intentional delays in the Mass are opportunities for prayer and offering.**
5. **Prepare before Mass: reconcile, confess, read readings, fast, dress reverently, arrive early.**
6. **The Mass has five readings, not three.**
7. **The Sign of Peace is about forgiveness, not just friendliness.**
8. **The dismissal sends us forth to live and share the Gospel.**
